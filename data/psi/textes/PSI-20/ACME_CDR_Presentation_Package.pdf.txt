BRE 
 
 
 
 
• Burning Rate Emulator 
• Jim Quintiere & Peter Sunderland (both U. Maryland) 
• Concept 
• Flat-plate burner with gaseous fuel flow simulating the 
combustion of condensed fuels 
• Heat flux to the burner is measured, where the flux 
vaporizes condensed-phase fuels 
• Fuels to be simulated include paper, plastic, alcohols 
• Goals  
• Quantify the ignition and extinction behavior of 
condensed fuels in microgravity. 
• Quantify the effects of burning at enhanced oxygen 
conditions in microgravity. 
• Why? 
• Spacecraft fire prevention through improved 
assessment for materials selection

CLD Flame 
 
 
 
 
• Coflow Laminar Diffusion Flame 
• Marshall Long & Mitchell Smooke (both Yale U.) 
• Concept 
• Lifted coflow (2D) flame, so little/no heat loss to burner 
• enabling improved modeling of the flame 
• Goals  
• Generation of modified kinetic mechanisms for 
hydrocarbon fuels that are able to model effectively 
diffusion flame structure under a larger parameter 
range than existing mechanisms. 
• Development of submodels for soot formation that are 
capable of predicting both high and low soot loading 
levels in hydrocarbon flames of various fuels. 
• Why? 
• Improved design capability through validation of  
combustion models over wider parameter range

E-FIELD Flames 
 
 
 
 
• Electric-Field Effects on Laminar Diffusion Flames 
• Derek Dunn-Rankin (UC Irvine), Felix Weinberg (Imperial 
College, London), Zeng-Guang Yuan (NCSER at NASA 
Glenn) 
• Concept 
• Gas-jet or coflow (2D) flame with high-voltage mesh (up 
to ±10 kV) electrode above (downstream of) burner 
• Goal 
• Understand chemi-ionization behavior and the resulting 
ion driven winds sufficiently well so that electrical 
properties of flames can be used to characterize (by 
monitoring ion current) and control them (via direct 
chemical or local convective influences). 
• Why? 
• Advanced control capability enabling improved 
combustion performance

Flame Design 
• A novel approach to clean, efficient diffusion flames 
• Rich Axelbaum (Washington U., St. Louis), Beei-Huan 
Chao (U. Hawaii), Peter Sunderland (U. Maryland), 
David L. Urban (NASA Glenn) 
• Concept 
• Spherical (1D) flame with nitrogen exchange 
• Goals  
• Evaluate the effects of flame structure on soot 
inception and flame extinction 
• Obtain a correlation of sooting limits with 
stoichiometric mixture fraction, Zst, and adiabatic 
temperature, Tad 
• Determine the importance of gas-phase oxidation on 
soot inception for high Zst flames 
• Demonstrate the existence of steady spherical flames 
• Identify pseudo-flammability limits for diffusion flames 
in terms of  Zst and Tad 
• Why? 
• To reduce soot and NOx through nitrogen exchange

Statement of Concern Recommended Action Closure Rationale 
One of the criteria for PDR is a preliminary limited 
life items list. Just because other experiments may 
not have such a list does not mean ACME should not 
have such a list. ACME presents a unique situation 
where experiments will occur in the same chamber 
as the Cube is located as well as tubing, wiring, 
cooling lines, etc. The project should to start 
identifying their materials lists from the Computer-
Aided Design (CAD) drawings so that compatibility 
issues/soft goods shelf life/limited life items can be 
identified early in the design cycle. Maybe testing 
would also be needed as well for materials with 
unknown compatibility? 
 
Identify the material 
compatibility and shelf life limits 
for the soft goods used in 
ACME and CIR 
 
It has been determined by the 
project that a materials analysis 
would not be done for PDR due to 
funding and resource limitations.  
The plan is to have this analysis 
and MIUL completed at CDR. 
  
Although the analysis was not 
complete at PDR, the ACME 
project is aware of potential issues 
with the Data Cube and other 
electronics and their compatibility 
with the gaseous fuels.  These 
issues are currently being tracked 
in the Risk Management 
Implementation Tool database.

RFA # 03 
Originator: Al Linne 
Originator Concurrence:  7 November 2013 
Statement of 
Concern 
Recommended Action Closure Rationale 
There are no warnings to 
eliminate damage to the 
test hardware 
To minimize the likelihood of 
hardware damage, the flight & 
ground software designs 
should incorporate software 
requirements to identify invalid 
commands and command 
parameters that are outside of 
allowable limits and to alert 
operations personnel that 
invalid commands were 
entered. 
 
A complete software command list for the ACME hardware has 
been derived from operational requirements for the ACME 
system.  Operational limits have been defined for each 
command and incorporated into the software requirement set.  
The software requirements are documented in the Software 
section of the ACME Engineering Requirements Document 
(ERD) ACME-REQ-001.   
This list of signals is also documented in the ACME Data 
Dictionary Document (ACME-DOC-008).   
These documents will be available for NASA review and 
comment during the CDR.   
The approved operational limitations will be built into the 
ACME ground command station software at the TSC.  Every 
command sent by an operator will be checked against these 
established limits.  Commands that violate safe ranges will be 
rejected (on the ground) and the operator will receive an error 
message.  This ground software will be verified, as part of the 
overall ACME test and verification  campaign, to ensure no 
command can ever reach the ACME hardware that is outside 
of these safely established limits.

Depress / Repress 
• Unless part of a pressurized system, all volumes are designed to 
adequately vent due to station / vehicle depressurization 
• Vented Containers will be verified by the stored energy method (SDP 
STD-ACME-1 17.a) where the volume has a stored energy < 3,063 ft-
lbs.  Volumes with a stored energy > 3,063 ft-lbs will have a depress 
rate calculated. 
 
• Depress rates are calculated* for each volume to show that the 
volume’s depress rate is greater than maximum station / vehicle 
depress rate so no pressure load is generated 
• Maximum depress rate is defined in section 3.1.1.2.1.5 part B of SSP-
50835 Rev C as 13.3kPa/s (1.93psi/sec) 
 
 
 
*AIAA Journal, October 1986, Vol. 24, No. 10, pg 1709-1711, “Formulas for Venting or Charging 
Gas from a Single Volume”, H.T. Yang

1. This hazard report works in conjunction with CIR hazard report FCF-C07, “Adiabatic Combustion

contents of a bottle were to be released into the CIR Combustion Chamber, any adiabatic combustion 
would result in a pressure less than the MDP of the CIR Combustion chamber.  The MDP of the CIR 
chamber is 135 psia. 
3. The CIR bottle leakage hazard controls and verifications are covered in CIR hazard report FCF-C08 
Leakage of Pressurized System.

Note (on hazard report) :   
1. This hazard report works in conjunction with CIR hazard report FCF-C07, “Adiabatic Combustion 
ruptures the chamber walls, causing damage to station and/or injury to crew.“  Overpressurizaton 
due to combustion will be controlled by limiting the oxygen volume in each gas bottle.  The CIR 
Valve Timers will be placed in the oxygen mode.  The chamber will be pre-filled with gaseous fuel 
and gaseous oxygen will flow through the ACME burner. 
2. CIR is responsible for filling the experiment gas bottles.  The ACME detonation analyses determine 
the limits on oxygen volumes in each bottle.  The oxygen gas volumes will be chosen such that 
even if the entire contents of a bottle were to be released into the CIR Combustion Chamber, any 
adiabatic combustion would result in a pressure less than the MDP of the CIR Combustion 
chamber.  The MDP of the CIR chamber is 135 psia.

C08 Leakage of Pressurized System.
