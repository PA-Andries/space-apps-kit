--- page 21 ---
Soot and Electric Fields
➢ Soot particle charge
▪ Soot particles may be originally charged, especially if they are formed 
through an ionic route 
▪ Soot particles gain a net charge through ion-attachment 
▪ Measurements indicate that in the hottest zone, soot particles may be 
charged to equilibrium, but this is not always the case and the initial 
particle charge seems to depend upon the material burnt 
▪ Net charge of soot particles in flames is generally positive even in the 
flame region where a large excess of electrons exist
▪ Soot particle primary size is found to be ~20 nm in non-electric flames; 
size can be reduced in electric fields
➢ Electric field effects on soot in laminar diffusion flames
▪ Ion wind can induce partial premixing (changes reaction zone)
▪ Ion precursors can be removed or reduced in number
▪ Soot residence time can be changed (increased or decreased)

Microgravity Justification
• High Quality Microgravity -- confounding effects of 
buoyancy
– Ion driven wind velocity approximately equal to buoyant wind 
velocity so convection and mixing effects are difficult to separate
– Complex flow patterns created in 1-g
– Stability of weak flames (liftoff and blowout) – as in CLD Flame
• Extended Duration -- time scales to reproducible state
– Electrical response (RC time constant)
– Ion transport time
– Ion wind convection
– Soot formation and transport
– Flow field establishment
– Thermal inertia effects

Experimental Objectives
Understand flame ion production and use ions to control 
diffusion flames by modifying local convection (via the ion 
wind) and local species transport (via direct ion drift)
A) Determine the relationship between electric field voltage and 
chemi-ion current as functions of total fuel flow rate and the 
amount of inert in the fuel.
B) Obtain the time response of flame chemiluminescence, luminosity, 
and ion current to large step changes in voltage, as functions of 
total fuel flow rate and amount of inert added to the fuel stream.
C) Evaluate and control sooting behavior (i.e., soot volume fraction 
and location of luminous soot) in relation to the electric field 
voltage and chemi-ion current as functions of total fuel flow rate 
and amount of inert added to the fuel stream.
D) Use the variation of the chemi-ion current as a function of applied 
voltage to characterize and control the combustion behavior of 
near-limit lifted flames in a coflow burner.
