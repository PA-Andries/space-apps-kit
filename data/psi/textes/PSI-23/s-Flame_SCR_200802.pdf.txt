s-Flame Merits of One-Dimensional Flames 
for Fundamental Studies
• Flame structure fundamentally affected by 
aerodynamics.
• Simple flow and flame configuration facilitate 
experimentation, computation, analysis, and 
comparison, with enhanced accuracy and reliability.
• Reduced effort in describing flows allows more 
detailed study of other aspects of flames such as 
chemistry.
• A one-dimensional flame is the simplest possible 
configuration.

s-Flame
II. Flame Extinction: 
Experimental, Low Da
• Fuel/inert mixtures will be issued from the spherical burner and 
ignited at initial flow rates expected (or known/observed) to form 
flames that evolve toward idealized steady-state conditions
• Following ignition and initial flame front movement, the fuel/inert 
flow rate will be abruptly reduced to a second flow rate below the 
expected extinction limit
• Quasi-steady extinction is expected to occur as the flame adjusts to 
the reduced flow rate
• To refine the determination of the extinction flow rate for each 
fuel/diluent mixture, repeated tests are to be conducted at 
successively lower initial flow rates and higher secondary flow 
rates.

s-Flame II. Flame Extinction: 
Experimental, High Da
• Fuel/inert mixtures will be issued from the spherical burner 
and ignited at flow rates slightly above and beyond that of 
the radiation extinction limit.
• Upon ignition the flame will grow and subsequently 
extinguish at the radiation extinction limit. 
• Rates of radiative emissions from the flame and radiative 
reabsorption elsewhere are to be accentuated in some cases 
by using a radiatively active diluent such as CO2.

s-Flame III. Flamefront Instabilities: 
Phenomenology
• Stoichiometry constraint and lack of chemistry render 
diffusion flame sheets absolutely stable.
• However, chemistry becomes important for near-limit 
burning. Will flame become unstable? 
• Recent theories & experiments show instability 
characteristics similar to those of premixed flames for 
near-limit burning
• Since (positive) stretch suppresses cellular instability 
while promotes pulsating instability, the (stretchless) 
spherical flame is well suited for such studies.

Complexities in 
Description of Chemistry
• Fuel oxidation is highly nonlinear and nonmonotonic in terms 
of temperature, pressure, and species concentrations
• Oxidation mechanisms consist of large number of species and 
reactions
• Characteristic reaction rates span over many orders of 
magnitude, hence computationally stiff
• Multicomponent diffusive transport can also be 
computationally expensive

The Role of mg Spherical Diffusion Flame in 
Comprehensive Mechanism Development
• Substantial research on homogeneous systems (shock 
tubes, flow reactors, etc.) and premixed flames
• Diffusion flame experiments mostly involve counter-
and stagnation flows, and droplet flames
• Burner-supported spherical flame is 1D and steady state
• Experiments of interest to chemistry are those involving 
ignition and extinction.
• Experiments for situations away from extinction would 
yield information on the diffusion and radiation 
parameters.

Experimental Approach  (4/6)
• Objective B – Quasi-Steady Extinction Limits, Low Da
–At the low Da extinction limit, chemical kinetic rates are 
retarded with respect to flow rates by inert gas in the 
fuel stream that decreases the flame temperature.
–Based on theoretical predictions, fuel/inert mixtures are 
to be chosen for which the extinction flame radius is 
large enough to minimize the effects of heat loss to the 
burner.
–The surface temperature of the burner will be directly 
measured to quantify this loss.

Experimental Approach  (5/6)
• Objective B – Quasi-Steady Extinction Limits, High Da
–Fuel/inert mixtures will be issued from the spherical

expected (or known/observed) to form steady flames 
when radiative loss is considered.  
–The quasi-steady extinction is expected to occur as the

steady-state radius.
–Rates of radiative emissions from the flame and radiative 
reabsorption elsewhere are to be accentuated in some 
cases by using a radiatively active diluent such as CO2.

Experimental Approach  (6/6)
• Objective C – Flame Front Instabilities
–Pulsating flame front instabilities are predicted to occur 
near the extinction conditions at both low and high Da.
–The approach taken to observe the instabilities is similar to 
the approach for observing extinction except fuel/diluent 
mixtures with large system Lewis numbers (Le) are used.
• Low Da: moderate initial flow rate, abrupt change to flow rate 
below predicted extinction rate.
• High Da: initial flow rate above predicted extinction flow rate 
with radiation.
• Instabilities are expected to appear before extinction.
–Radiating diluents are used to further encourage the 
instabilities.
