2.2 SAME Experiment Background 
Since the very beginning of the manned space flight program, NASA engineers and scientists have gone to great lengths to 
prevent fires from occurring on spacecraft. They have done this by adapting fire safety systems and procedures developed 
on Earth to the unique conditions and operating environment of spacecraft. However, microgravity combustion experiments 
have shown that fire can behave very differently in space and the best methods to prevent, detect, and extinguish fires on a 
spacecraft may be very different than those used on Earth. 
In 2000, NASA's Bioastronautics Initiative began with the objective of ensuring and enhancing the health, safety, and 
performance of humans in space. As part of this effort, the  Life Support and Habitation Branch at NASA Glenn Research 
Center is responsible for developing and implementing a research plan to significantly improve fire safety on spacecraft and 
in space habitats. This goal will be achieved by applying a significant amount of fundamental knowledge and the 
experimental capability of microgravity research to spacecraft fire safety questions. 
Spacecraft smoke detectors must detect smoke with a variety of particulate types. Hydrocarbon fuels typically produce soot, 
while overheated plastics produce droplets of recondensed polymer fragments. Paper and silicone rubber produce a smoke 
that is composed of liquid droplets of recondensed pyrolysis products. Differences in the nucleation and growth processes 
for these different types of smokes create particles that have different sizes and properties. 
The Comparative Soot Diagnostics (CSD) experiment that flew in the Glovebox on STS-75 demonstrated that the  smokes 
with solid particulates created in microgravity formed longer chains, and aggregated into larger particles than those created 
in normal Earth gravity. This is surmised to have occurred because of the increased residence time in the high concentration 
smoke regions and to have affected the responses of the two spacecraft smoke detectors (ISS and STS) utilized in that 
experiment. 
To develop smoke detectors that are sensitive to the type and size of smoke particles that will be encountered in 
microgravity and to allow them to distinguish between dust and smoke, better knowledge of the size distribution and 
morphology of the particulates is required. 
2.3 SAME Experiment Objectives 
The major goal of this experiment is to quantify the sizes that the liquid smoke particulates achieve in microgravity and to 
relate the droplet size and distribution to the smoke generation and smoke transport conditions in the experiment. This will 
be accomplished by extending the Comparative Soot Diagnostics (CSD) concept to provide a particulate count and mass 
density determination to the diagnostic array used to analyze these liquid smokes. The experimental design and practical 
application of this data will be enhanced by the development of a numerical code that will predict the smoke droplet growth 
as a function of the fuel pyrolysis rate, the thermodynamic properties of the pyrolysis vapor, and the flow environment. 
The SAME experiment has the following (listed) objectives: 
1. Make measurements of three moments of the particle size distribution for the μg smoke from several 
materials found in spacecraft considering the effects of sample temperature, air flow, smoke residence time 
(near the source) and smoke aging. The primary targets are materials that produce liquid smoke particulate 
but solid smoke producers will also be considered.  
2. Make measurements of the effect of µg and 1-g on the size distribution of liquid aerosol smokes (using an 
idealized smoke e.g. DBT) to provide data for numerical model comparison.   
3. Evaluate the performance in µg of the two existing U.S. spacecraft smoke detector designs for the test 
conditions. 
4. Evaluate other smoke detection/sensing devices at NASA’s request for the test materials  
2.4 SAME “Firsts” for NASA 
• Measurements of microgravity smoke: Diagnostics such as the P-Trak and the DustTrak will be used to measure 
particle count and particle mass concentration, respectively.  Both devices are manufactured by TSI.  With the aid of 
these instruments (and others), microgravity smoke will be characterized. 
• The use of rapid prototype materials for manned flight experiment hardware. 
• Use of an automatic, remotely adjustable, ‘particulate friendly’ dilution system.

2.5 The SAME Risk mitigation experiment – DAFT  
The objective for the risk mitigation experiment, DAFT (Dust and Aerosol Measurement Feasibility Test), is to provide a 
microgravity technology demonstration of the P-Trak Ultrafine Particle Counter manufactured by TSI.  The P-Trak is a 
hand held air quality monitor which is capable of counting particles whose mean diameter is in the range of 1.0 to 0.05 
microns. 
After extensive market research performed by both the Engineering and Science teams, it has been determined that the P-
Trak is the only commercially available hardware that can perform this particle count while maintaining the weight, 
resource, and space limitations for an MSG experiment.   
P-Trak measures particle number concentrations in real-time  while storing that data in a memory maintained by an on-
board battery. These measurements are made in units of particles per cubic centimeter (pt/cm3) versus traditional aerosol 
measurements of milligrams per cubic meter (mg/m3) made by photometers.  P-Trak operates by using the particles in the 
aerosol as seeds to condense droplets of alcohol (a condensate nuclei counter).  These droplets have many times the average 
diameter of the original particles, and may, therefore, be easily counted with standard optical techniques.  The potential 
problem is that these droplets are directed through a nozzle to facilitate the counting, and some of these droplets (a 
predictable percentage) collide with the walls and form a film. In a gravity environment, this film is recycled by ‘falling’ 
down hill. 
In microgravity, the distinct possibility exists that these droplets will form a film that will bridge and block the nozzle, 
effectively plugging the flow of droplets and preventing the device from functioning, or obscuring the optics.  Furthermore, 
there is some concern that the droplets inside the optics chamber may get trapped in an internal circular motion and be 
counted more than once, producing an erroneously high count. 
The modification that was implemented in order to counteract these potential problems was: 60. 0.006” grooves were 
machined down the length of the inside of the condenser.  These grooves were designed to provide a mechanism in which 
the smoke particle/ alcohol mixture can wick its way back to reservoir end to simulate the effects of gravity, hence, 
inhibiting the possibility of the fluid to inhibit the functioning of the unit. 
The modifications to account for and eliminate this problem have been devised and implemented, however, it is not possible 
to test the effectiveness of these modifications in any of the available ground based facilities and/or the KC-135 due to the 
duration of the nominal operation time required (minutes per instance).  This testing, and the verification of the accuracy of 
the instrument functions on orbit, are the core of the DAFT experiment. 
Two P-Trak units are included in DAFT flight hardware, one being only flight hardened, and the other with the modified 
condenser and flight hardened. 
To successfully accomplish P-Trak functional demonstration, additional hardware (which will not be explained in detail 
here) has to be delivered to the ISS. This hardware includes a dust concentration monitor (DustTrak), data cables, fill 
assembly, sample bag assemblies, sample holders, virtual impactor, alcohol reservoir, battery packs, a HEPA filter 
assembly, and appropriate tubing. 
The P-Trak utilizes isopropyl alcohol as a condensate, and its use must therefore, be carefully monitored and requires a 
specific agreement for that use on the ISS which is contained within a Volatile Usage Agreement (VUA) created for the 
experiment.

4.0 SAME CREW INTERFACES 
4.1 Hardware Subsystem Description 
The major planned components of SAME are as follows. They are organized by the assembly level, and the order in which 
they are currently planned to be installed into the MSG.: 
1. Fluids Control Box Assembly (67198MABA220) 
The Fluids Control Box (FCB) installs on the back wall of the MSG in the upper left section. It is a fan cooled 
enclosure made from aluminum that contains most of the fluids control devices and monitoring devices including 
solenoids, pressure regulator, pressure transducers and mass flow controller. It interfaces directly with the MSG 
fluids resources (VES and GN2) and the Power Distribution and Control Unit (PDCU), and distributes the fluids 
resources accordingly. Please refer to the SAME Fluids Schematic (67198PABA000) for more information on its, 
and the other fluids system functions. 
2. Sample Plate Assembly (Experiment Support Plate Assembly) 67198ABA100 
This assembly, when fully integrated, contains all of the major functional systems of SAME. It is a mounting plate 
which includes the mounting hardware for the Sample Carousel, the Thermal Precipitator Unit (TPU), the 
Dynamic Diagnostics Assembly (DDA) and the Aging Chamber Assembly (ACA). Permanently mounted to it 
are: 
• The Smoke Generation Tube 
• The Video Camera and Controller 
• The Sample Positioning system including: 
o the Sample Turning Module 
o the sample engagement  mechanism 
o the sample position indicator 
o the sample positioning lock mechanism 
o the Sample Carousel mounting system

3. Dynamic Diagnostics Assembly (DDA) (67198MDBA165) 
The DDA is attached to the Sample Plate Assembly and the Aging Chamber Assembly by the crew and contains: 
the ISS and STS smoke detectors and the sampling ports that supply the P-Trak Enclosure and the Commercial 
Diagnostics Enclosure (and port(s) added for any additional diagnostics required). The DDA has a very low 
internal volume, and the flow of the sample aerosol through it is controlled by regulating and balancing the 
requirements of the active diagnostics in the DDA with the exhaust rate of the Aging Chamber piston, as 
controlled by its mass flow controller. It also contains a port, protected by a HEPA filter, that is connected to the 
VES for exhaust and purging. 
4. Aging Chamber Assembly (67198MABA150) 
The Aging Chamber Assembly contains the cylindrical, metallic Aging Chamber, the pneumatically activated 
Inlet and Outlet Gate Valves and the Inlet and Outlet Elbows, which connect to the Smoke Generation Tube and 
the DDA, respectively. A Rulon piston, internal to the chamber, moves the sample into and out from the internal 
volume, and that flow is controlled by a mass flow controller which regulates the pressure or vacuum flow 
accordingly.  Sampling ports are provided in the elbows for use by the Thermal Precipitator, and the unit contains 
a Mixing Fan to insure that the contents are homogenous before disbursal to the DDA. 
5. Data Acquisition and Control Unit (DACU) (67198EABA660) 
The DACU is the central control unit for SAME. The baseline software is resident there, and, although many of 
the other packages contain their own relay and control cards, ultimately it is the DACU that interfaces with the 
Ethernet and the MLC to orchestrate the performance of the experiment. It also collects and stores data for 
subsequent downlink through the MSG systems, and controls the MSG Video Recorder to provide a taped record 
of the pyrolysis event. 
6. P-Trak Enclosure (67198MABA500) 
The P-Trak Enclosure is a flight hardened assembly containing a TSI P-Trak modified to take advantage of the 
VES system and to function in a microgravity environment. Although it interfaces with the DDA, the DACU, the 
Fluids Control Unit and the Power Distribution and Control Unit, it is basically a smart box that can operate 
independently and store its own data.  
7. Commercial Diagnostics Enclosure (CDE) (67198MABA600) 
The CDE houses two of the SAME diagnostics, the TSI DustTrak and a First Alert ionization smoke detector. 
Both units have been hardened for flight and have been modified to utilize the VES to drive the sample. The 
DustTrak has also been modified to utilize MSG air to provide its ‘sheath air’ in order to decrease the amount of 
aerosol required by the unit. Like P-Trak, the DustTrak portion of this device can operate independently and store 
its own data. The modified First Alert cannot.  
8. Dynamic Diluter (DD) (67198MABA180) 
The DD is a custom designed component that provides dilution of the aerosol from the DDA before its 
introduction into the P-Trak (and possibly the DustTrak). It works by utilizing a laminar flow element to measure 
the amount of sample being introduced and using that information (from a differential pressure transducer) to 
increase or decrease the diluent flow to achieve the desired concentration. The dilution setpoint may be altered by 
software and adjusts to the desired setting in approximately 5 seconds. 
9. Power Distribution and Control Unit (PDCU) (67198EABA680) 
The PDCU is the central disbursement point for the commands, power distribution and data collection for SAME. 
It contains the power converters, main relays and terminal data connections for the entire system and interfaces the 
DACU with the various systems and devices constituting the experiment.

10. Sample Carousel Assembly (67198MABB400) 
The Sample Carousel Assemblies (5) each contain six (6) Sample Tubes that interface the samples to the Smoke 
Generation Tube and allow the capture of the smoke in the Aging Chamber. Sample Carousels are sequentially 
installed onto the mounting locations on the Sample Plate Assembly, and the mechanisms contained by it identify 
the individual sample numbers, rotate the carousel to the correct position and engage the Sample Tube, which 
allows the sample to be in a position to be energized.  The samples themselves are composed of five (5) materials: 
Teflon, Kapton, Silicone, Cellulose and DiButyl Phthalate (which being a liquid, is contained within a small 
section of cotton lampwick) in small, cylindrical sections, that are wrapped with coils of wire (baseline: stainless 
steel) that both provides the heat energy to the sample when energized and measures its temperature (by its 
varying resistance). 
These samples are removed from the tubes for weighing, both before and after the experiment (before launch and 
after being downmassed) to quantify the actual mass loss during pyrolysis. 
11. Thermal Precipitator Unit (TPU) (67198MABB300) 
The TPU is a device that captures actual samples of the aerosol particulate on Transmission Electron Microscope 
(TEM) grids for examination when returned from the ISS. The units (4) are number matched to the samples in the 
carousel, to take samples from both the Inlet and Outlet Elbows leading to and from the Aging Chamber. 
12. Alcohol Wick Storage Container (67198MABD700) 
The Alcohol Wick Storage Container is a custom manufactured aluminum vessel to contain the alcohol wick 
required by the P-Trak during the time (launch, non-ops) when it is not in direct use. Only one is nominally 
required, but two are included to provide one as a backup. 
13. Hoses 
SAME utilizes ISS GN2 and the VES system both as resources for the experiment process and as systems to 
power its devices. These resources are distributed to the components from the FCU via the hoses, which are 
nominally operated at +/- 15 psig. In order to conserve weight and volume, and provide ease of operation, acrylic 
hoses and quick disconnects will be used for the distribution of pressure and vacuum throughout the system (not 
typical Teflon with stainless steel overbraid). 
14. Harnessing 
All harnessing will conform to applicable standards and be designed to minimize the crew time needed to make 
the required experiment connections, without sacrificing reliability. 
15. SAME Service Kit 
This is a place holder. SAME has not yet defined any servicing requirements. 
4.2 Video Recording Devices 
Video recordings of the smoke evolution process are very important for establishing proper operation of the test apparatus 
and for debugging the system operation.  Furthermore, the video provides a helpful demonstration of the variability in 
smoke evolution. Experience with CSD showed dramatic cases where clearly visible smoke streams were not always 
detectable by the smoke detectors while invisible smoke streams were readily detected. Normal video resolution is 
acceptable if the smoke is illuminated by forward scattered light from an appropriate light source. Video will be viewed on 
and recorded by the MSG video Monitor/Recorder. 
4.3 On-Orbit Crew Operational Functions 
The crew will be asked to assemble the experiment from provided subsections, load software, reload Sample Carousels, 
Thermal Precipitator Modules, and alcohol for the particle counter, enter parameters, initiate the experiment, and 
disassemble the experiment.

5.0 SAME SOFTWARE CONTROL 
5.1 Software Controlled Operational Functions 
The DACU is the central point for SAME control.  It is ultimately responsible for all experiment operations and interfacing 
with the environment external to SAME.  It contains all of the logic necessary to autonomously operate the experiment as 
well as receive, validate, and process commands from the MSG.  The DACU periodically transmits the status of the Smoke 
system to the MSG, which then forwards it to the ground.   The software can autonomously control the progress of 
preprogrammed test, manage and store all science and system files on the local data storage medium, control the MSG 
video recorder capturing Smoke images, and copy the files to the MLC for downlink.  The software inside the DACU is 
also responsible for providing diagnostic information and manual control of all sensors and controls using the GSE port. 
Please refer to the SAME Baseline Software Requirements Document (SAME-DOC-004) for more detail. 
5.2 Data Collection 
Data is collected and stored in the DACU until it is time to downlink the information.  The Thermal Precipitator Module, 
which collects samples of the smoke, will be bagged and returned for analysis.  Sample Carousels are retained so that the 
samples can be weighed after return to Earth. 
6.0 SAME OPERATIONS 
6.1 Operations Flow 
1. Experiment Setup- The experiment will be shipped in individual subassemblies that will be assembled inside of 
the MSG.  Parts will be inserted through the MSG side ports starting with the upper, back components.  After 
the MSG ports are covered and the rack is returned to nominal position, the Experiment Software is installed 
onto the MSG Laptop. 
2. Perform Experiment- The first steps in performing the experiment are to load the desired Sample Carousel, load 
or replenish the alcohol wick in the particle counter, and install a new Thermal Precipitator Module.  Next, the 
start-up check out routine is performed.  After the uplink parameters are sent, the experiment is initiated.  Then, 
the baseline uplink parameters are modified, as required, for the next test and the experiment is initiated again 
until all six samples in the Sample Carousel have been used.  The next number matched Sample Carousel and 
Thermal Precipitator Module will then be loaded and the experiment will continue as such until complete. 
3. Shutdown and Repack Experiment- When all the Sample Carousels have been used and all the experiments 
have been performed,  the final Sample Carousel and Thermal Precipitator Module will be capped and bagged.  
In reverse order of installation, the experiment hardware will be repackaged for downmassing. 
6.2 Detailed Operations 
Please refer to the SAME Operations Concept Document (SAME-DOC-009) for a more detailed description of all 
Operations functions.

SAME will follow the standard ISS procedures for scheduling activities aboard the station including OCR and iURC

7.0 SAFETY CONCERNS & PREVENTATIVE MEASURES 
7.1 Overall Strategy 
Safety analysis is being performed for the SAME (formerly Smoke) hardware in accordance with the safety policies and 
safety requirements for flight hardware (NSTS1700.7B and ISS Addendum). This assessment is being prepared using 
NSTS 13830C as a guideline for data submittal. Hazards not eliminated by design are addressed in either the latest revision 
of standardized hazard control report JSC Form 1230 for ISS and launch/landing phase of the mission or in a Unique 
Hazard Report, JSC 542B. For the most part, the SAME experiment uses generic hazard controls for electrical power 
hazards, sharp edges, EMI (testing), rotating equipment (smaller than 8 inch diameter rotors and less than 8000 RPM), Fire 
Detection and suppression, and touch temperature. More detail can be found in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001).

7.2 Pressurized Systems  
The pressure system includes the aging chamber, the system for moving the piston in the aging chamber (valves, regulator, 
lines, hoses, relief devices) and the Dynamic Diluter. All of the parts will be designed to meet the requirements of 
NSTS1700.7 ISS addendum, NASA-STD-5003, SSP 30558, and SSP 52005 for pressurized hardware. Pressurized lines 
and fittings will be designed to a factor of safety of 4.0 X System MDP of 200 psid (ISS N2 limiting pressure). Pressurized 
system components will be designed to a factor of safety of greater than or equal to 2.5 X System MDP of 200 psid. Down 
stream of the pressure reduction the MDP is 20 psig. These components are designed with the same factors of safety. See 
table of components and factors of safety and hazard report SMOKE-04. Materials will be selected and evaluated for stress 
corrosion and/or fluid compatibility in accordance with SSP 30233, Space Station Requirements for Materials and 
Processes. In the event of loss of power all solenoids will close including the mass flow controllers, therefore the system will 
not over pressurize. More detail can be found in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001) specifically hazard report 
SMOKE-04. 
7.3 Structures 
The SAME experiment is launched in stowage and contained in MSG for on orbit use. The structural aspect of SAME is 
primarily sealed containers and vented containers. Pressure systems are covered in section 1.2. SAME structural failure is 
addressed in Hazard Report SMOKE STD 01 in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001). 
7.4 Materials 
The ZIN Tech Engineering Team will assure that reasonable measures have been taken with regard to material selection for 
the experiment‘s design, and selected materials are safe for the intended manned space flight application in their respective 
usage environment. A material flammability assessment will be performed per NSTS  22648. The flight unit will be offgas 
tested. The SAME Project Team will prepare the standard Materials Identification & Usage List (MIUL) and MUA’s. ZIN 
Engineering will review and approve material evaluation prepared by the SAME Project, and submit it to 
NASA/GRC/SMAD for concurrence. Upon material evaluation approval, NASA/GRC/SMTD will issue the Material 
Certification Letter in accordance with the guidelines established in the  applicable Material and Processes (M&P) 
Intercenter Agreement prior to flight hardware fabrication. Further information can be found in Hazard Report SMOKE 
STD 01 in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001). 
7.5 Frangible Materials (Glass) 
All of the hardware which contains glass will undergo a vibration test in the stowed configuration at workmanship or 
protoflight levels and a post-test visual inspection to verify ability to survive launch loads. The duct with the video camera 
lens and LEDs installed will be stowed with clear plastic for crew inspection after launch. 
7.6 Release of Experiment By-products into the Cabin Atmosphere 
The P-Trak needs a cartridge with an alcohol-saturated wick for its operation; therefore, a risk of contaminating the ISS 
environment has to be considered.  The amount of alcohol released will be carefully controlled and limited. The Project 
Team will submit a Volatile Usage Agreement request to JSC/OC (Material and Processes) for disposition and approval.  
All payloads are to submit their usage requests to a single approval authority at JSC because alcohol usage is a cumulative 
risk, and it will be necessary to process VUA through a single approval authority to track the total quantity of alcohol being 
used. More detail can be found in the safety date package (SMOKE-SDP-001). 
The pyrolysis products of Teflon currently have a JSC Tox level 3 for 5 minutes after creation. After this point the particles 
coalesce and drop to tox level 0. A hazard report has been generated to address this issue. The smoke is contained in the 
smoke tube and aging chamber for 5 minutes. All the smoke is expelled through HEPA filters, some of which dumps into 
the ISS vent the rest of it into the MSG. The MSG is the second containment of the smoke if the aging chamber where to 
leak. Finally the MSG is under a negative pressure which would prevent the smoke from leaking in the event of an MSG 
leak. Hazard Report SAME-08 has been written and submitted to the PSRP for review.

7.7 Non-Ionizing Laser Radiation 
The ISS Smoke Detector, the P-Trak and the DustTrak contain lasers as a part of their diagnostics systems.  These lasers are 
of Class 1 lasers, and the laser beams are fully contained. These items are not removed from their original housings and are 
used in the applications for which they are intended. There is no crew access to the lasers at any time.  The lasers meet 
ANSI Z136.1-2000 standards for Class 1. More detail can be found in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001).  
7.8 Ionizing Radiation 
The STS Smoke detector and the First Alert Smoke Alarm each contain 1 micro curie of Americium-241.  These items are 
not removed from their original housings and are used in the applications for which they are intended. More detail can be 
found in the safety date package (SAME-SDP-001). 
8.0 PRODUCT ASSURANCE 
In order to assure the overall quality and performance of the systems created by the project team, Product Assurance will be 
provided by the ZIN Quality team and will include but not be limited to oversight of the manufacturing and assembly

found in the following documents: SAME Product Assurance Plan (SAME-PLN-003) and Same Software Assurance Plan
