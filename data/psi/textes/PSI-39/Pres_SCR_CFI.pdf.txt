FLEX Experiment Summary 
• Original of FLEX experiments was to observe 
and systematically study hot flame ignition and 
extinction in microgravity 
• A new phenomenon where a hot flame extinction 
leads to a low-temperature, “cool-flame” burning 
in microgravity observed for the first time 
• Cool flames are prevalent in FLEX experiments 
o Strong function of ambient pressure 
o Strong function of ambient environment – 
ambient oxygen mole fraction and diluent 
o Function of fuel composition 
• No systematic study of cool flames and their 
presence inferred and not observed

Experiment Objectives – Cool Combustion, CFI 
1. Further understanding of the combustion characteristics of normal alkanes, 
particularly in the low temperature region by conducting droplet combustion 
experiments in low gravity with fuels that supports cool flame burning and 
extinction. 
2.  Investigate the low temperature burning behavior of droplets consisting of pure 
fuels and bio-fuel constituents (and mixtures of them), as well as surrogate 
reference fuels to determine the relationship between the cool flame burning 
characteristics in microgravity droplet combustion and the octane/cetane 
behavior of the fuel. 
3.  Explore the low temperature chemistry of alkanes further by mixing additives to 
the fuel that disrupt the low temperature chemical pathways.  [Ground-based 
only]

• Spherical symmetry requires g-levels less than 10-4g0

• Drop towers have appropriate g-levels

• Proposed experiments involve:

4. Cool-flame burning (5 – 60 seconds) 
5. Cool-flame extinction 
• Long-duration microgravity facilities are required for detailed parametric study

Requirements – Test Fuels 
• Objective 1: Study the ignition, hot-flame burning and extinction, cool flame 
burning and cool flame extinction of a model normal alkane fuel. 
o Normal dodecane 
• Objective 2: Investigate the low temperature burning of pure fuels, mixtures of 
pure fuels that are surrogates for real fuels and bio-fuel constituents to

flame burning characteristics.

Requirements – Ambient Environment 
• Pressure 
o Strongly influences cool flame burning and extinction – both hypo- and 
hyper-baric pressures 
o 0.5 – 3.0 atm required, up to 5 atm desired (hardware limit) 
• Oxygen Mole Fraction 
o 0.10 – 0.40, similar to the range in FLEX experiments 
• Ambient Diluent 
o Primarily nitrogen (Objective 1 and 2) 
o Helium and xenon (Objective 1) 
Ø Strongly influence cool flame burning and extinction 
• Misc. Ambient Requirements 
o Similar to FLEX requirements 
o Ambient humidity less than 10% (FLEX had lower requirement for 
methanol) 
o g-levels ≤ 10-5g0

--- page 9 ---
Requirements – Droplet Imaging 
• Measure droplet size and shape, droplet dynamics, soot volume fraction, etc. as 
functions of time 
• No change to camera or backlight for new experiment necessary 
• Increase FOV from 30 mm (FLEX) to 50 mm (CFI) 
o Increases success rate by allowing for greater free deployment droplet drift 
o FLEX requires drift less than 1 mm/sec to stay in FOV 
o Reduces spatial resolution (30 µm to 50 µm)

--- page 10 ---
Requirements – Hot Flame Imaging 
• Currently measure either OH* or CH chemiluminescence using intensified 
black and white camera 
o OH* (310 nm) images very dim, required pixel binning (2x2) to get good 
images, but reduced spatial resolution 
o CH (430 nm) images have improved contrast and reduced noise – possible 
to eliminate binning (increases data storage/downlink requirements) 
• Increase FOV from 50 mm (FLEX) to 80 mm (CFI) 
o Increases success rate by allowing for greater droplet drift 
• Current camera configuration acceptable for hot-flame imaging 
o Unlikely to be able to perform chemiluminescence of both the hot-flame 
and cool flame with current camera configuration 
• Require the capability to image the hot-flame 
o Either OH* or CH (preferred) chemiluminescence 
o 100 µm spatial resolution 
o 30 fps 
o Adjustable gain required (almost impossible to know appropriate gain 
based on ground-based testing)

--- page 11 ---
Requirements – Cool Flame Imaging 
• Currently have no way to image cool flames on CIR/MDCA 
o Removing/changing filter and/or increasing gain on LLUV possible, no 
way to shield the camera from ignition or hot flame 
• Cool flame expected to be approximately ½ size of hot flame 
o FOV , spatial resolution and framing rate requirements similar to hot flame 
o Can use the same camera for both hot and cool flame imaging, but spectral 
ranges are different and required gain is much different 
• Based on literature search and FLEX radiometer data, cool flames are expected 
to be very dim

Requirements – Color Camera Imaging 
• Necessary for droplet deployment, hardware diagnostics, etc. 
• Closest thing to being able to look into the chamber. 
• FLEX color camera with internal chamber light is acceptable for CFI 
o Zoom capability is critical.  Zoom-in for droplet dispense, hardware 
diagnostics, igniter positioning.  Zoom-out to view droplet burn. 
o Will not be able to image the cool flame 
o Must be able to image the vapor cloud and hot flame. 
o Must monitor radiation damage to image array over time 
• Require downlink of this view (recorded at TSC) during experiment.  Also 
record on CIR because of image quality loss during ISS video compression).

Requirements – Flame Radiometer 
• Original FLEX radiometers not sensitive enough to adequately 
detect dim (hot) flames and had unacceptably low SNR 
• Starting in 2011, FLEX testing used replacement radiometers 
o Broadband: 0.6 – 10.0 µm sensitivity, images hot flame 
o Narrowband: 5.0 – 6.5 µm ( H2O ), sensitive to cool flame 
o 10 Hz frequency response, sampled at 100 Hz 
o Fixed gain, not sensitive to both hot and cool flames 
• Require flame radiometric measurements for CFI 
o Same wavelength bands as current radiometer package 
o Require auto-gain so each radiometer can capture both hot 
and cool flame 
Ø ~ 0.1 – 100 W flame radiance range 
Ø May not be able to capture flame radiance during re-
ignition 
• Require at least 10 Hz sampling (same as FLEX) 
• Same FOV as FLEX radiometers

Experiment Procedures and Test Matrix 
• Experiment procedures similar to FLEX experiments 
o FLEX looked for flammability limits and did a few tests in gradually 
decreasing oxygen ambients 
o CFI will examine fewer ambients, but more tests in each ambient 
• Exact test matrix will be determined based on further analysis of FLEX data and 
companion theoretical/numerical analysis 
o Test matrix in SRD used for resource estimation 
o Require interactive experimentation, i.e., future experiments guided by the 
results of the current experiments 
Ø Requires timely downlink of experimental data. 
o Requires detailed interaction with engineering team 
Ø Tests planned to optimize resource utilization 
• Pressure range: 0.5 – 3.0 atm required (5.0 atm desired) 
• Ambient oxygen mole fraction:  0.10 – 0.40 
• Ambient gas mixture: O2/N2, O2/He, O2/Xe, tertiary mixtures possible
